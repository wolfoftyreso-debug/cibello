Quick dinner ideas: on the table in 20 to 30 minutes

A quick dinner is only quick if you already have the ingredients. This guide collects dishes that nearly always work from a normal fridge and cupboard, shows how to keep them from all turning into pasta, and explains how Cibello finds a suggestion from what is actually in your kitchen.

Why quick dinners go wrong

Most recipes labelled quick assume you have everything to hand. In real life a couple of things are usually missing, and 20 minutes becomes 50 with a trip to the shop. The other common problem is that all the quick dishes end up the same: pasta, pasta and pasta again. The fix for both is to let the suggestions start from what you actually have, and to let something other than your memory keep track of the variety.

There is a third trap, too: quick dinners that need a long shopping list of small, specific items. A dish that takes 20 minutes but requires three fresh herbs and a jar you will use once is not a weeknight dish. The ones below rely on things you probably keep anyway.

What these dishes share is that they tolerate change. No chicken? Beans will do. No pepper? A carrot works. Cibello shows which ingredients you have and which are missing, so you can see in a moment whether a recipe works as written or needs adapting.

A dinner suggestion in under a minute

  1. Photograph the fridge and cupboards. The app recognises the items and builds your food inventory. You check the result before saving it, since AI can misread a label.
  2. Ask what to eat and browse suggestions that start from what is at home and what should be used first.
  3. Pick from the dishes where nearly all the ingredients are already in the kitchen.
  4. Add anything missing to the shopping list, or choose something else.

The suggestions also weigh in what is about to expire. A quick dinner that rescues the cream with two days left on it is doubly useful. Read more about best before and use by dates and about reducing food waste at home.

Vary the protein, not the dish

The dishes in the table are frameworks rather than recipes. The same stir-fry works with chicken one week, prawns the next and tofu the week after. The same tacos take mince, fish or black beans. If you think in frameworks, you can keep a short list of dishes everyone likes and still eat something different every night. Cibello learns what your household tends to pick and suggests variations that resemble your favourites but use what is in the kitchen right now.

Quick dinners with children at the table

With children, the quick dinner usually also needs to be recognisable. Tacos, pancakes, meatballs and pasta with tomato sauce work for a reason. The trick is to vary the sides and the protein rather than the dish itself, and to let children assemble their own plate where possible. More ideas are in our guide to family dinner ideas, and if you would rather plan a little further ahead, the weekly meal planner handles a week of quick weekday dishes at once.

When you have no idea at all

Some evenings the question is not how quickly but what. The dinner picker on our what to eat tonight page offers a suggestion from forty everyday dishes with no account needed. In the app the suggestions go one step further and start from what is actually in your fridge, with an explanation of why each dish is proposed. Either way, the aim is the same: to make the decision take less time than the cooking.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a quick dinner?

A quick dinner can be cooked in 20 to 30 minutes from ingredients you already have, without a trip to the shop first.

How do I find a quick dinner from what I have at home?

Register your fridge and cupboards in Cibello by photographing them. The app matches your inventory against recipes and shows dishes you can cook now, with what should be used first at the top.

Can I see what is missing from a recipe?

Yes. Cibello shows which ingredients are missing from each recipe. Always check ingredients and allergens yourself.

Which quick dinners suit children?

Tacos, pancakes, pasta with tomato sauce and meatballs tend to work. Vary the sides and the protein rather than the whole dish.
