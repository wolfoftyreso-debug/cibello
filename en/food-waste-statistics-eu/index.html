Food waste statistics for the EU: how much do we actually throw away?

This page collects the official Eurostat figures on food waste in the European Union: total tonnes, kilograms per inhabitant and which part of the chain wastes most, with a link to the source. It is updated when Eurostat publishes new estimates.

More than half of all food waste in the EU happens at home. That is an uncomfortable figure, but also an encouraging one: the largest part of the problem sits in habits that individual households can change, without waiting for industry or legislation. Part of those 69 kilograms is unavoidable, such as peel and bones. The rest is food bought with good intentions and then forgotten.

The order is not accidental either. At the start of the chain, businesses work with large volumes and tight planning; at home, people shop for a week that rarely goes exactly as expected. That is why the household is also the place where a small change in habits makes the biggest difference.

The EU target for 2030

With the 2025 revision of the Waste Framework Directive, the EU set binding food waste reduction targets for the first time. By 2030, food waste in processing and manufacturing is to fall by 10 %, and food waste in retail, restaurants, food services and households together is to fall by 30 % per capita. Both targets are measured against the average for 2021 to 2023. These are EU-wide targets; how each member state gets there is a national matter, and this page does not attempt to summarise national plans.

Figures by country

Alongside the EU total, Eurostat publishes figures for each member state, and national statistics offices publish their own country data, often with more detail on what households actually throw away. The two are not always directly comparable, because measurement methods and definitions vary from country to country. If you want to compare your own country with the EU average, the Eurostat tables are the safest place to do it, since one definition applies to everyone. This page deliberately stays at EU level. When you read national figures, check the reference year and whether inedible parts are included. For Sweden, this site has a separate page with national figures, matsvinn i Sverige, written in Swedish and based on the Swedish Environmental Protection Agency and the Swedish Food Agency.

What the figures mean for a household

Sixty-nine kilograms per person per year is a bag of shopping every week or so, much of it fruit, vegetables, bread and leftovers that were meant to be eaten. Surveys across Europe keep pointing to the same remedies: plan a few dinners at a time, shop from a list, store food properly and use what has the shortest date first. None of it is complicated; the difficulty is keeping it up when the week gets busy. Frequently asked questions

How much food is wasted in the EU each year?

About 58.2 million tonnes in 2023 according to Eurostat, or roughly 130 kg per inhabitant. Households account for 53 percent of the total.

How much food does a household throw away?

Eurostat puts household food waste at 69 kg per inhabitant per year, including inedible parts such as peel and bones.

What is the EU target for food waste?

By 2030, a 10 percent reduction in processing and manufacturing and a 30 percent reduction per capita in retail, restaurants, food services and households, compared with the 2021 to 2023 average.

Where can I find food waste figures for my country?

Eurostat publishes figures per member state, and national statistics offices publish their own country data. Check the reference year and definitions before comparing.
