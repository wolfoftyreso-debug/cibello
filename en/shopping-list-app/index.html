A shared shopping list that starts from what you already have

Most shopping lists are written from memory, by one person, and end up either too long or too short. Cibello's list starts from your food inventory and your planned meals, adds only what is missing and is shared with everyone in the household. Here is how it works and why it saves both money and food.

The trouble with ordinary shopping lists

A list written from memory tends to fail in one of two ways. Either it is too long, because you were not sure whether there was still rice at home, so you bought more. Or it is too short, because the one thing the recipe needed never made it onto the list. Shopping list apps solve the sharing problem, but not the connection to what is actually in the kitchen or what is going to be cooked. Someone still has to hold everything in their head, and that someone is usually the same person every week.

How the shopping list in Cibello works

  1. It starts from the inventory. When you plan a dish, its ingredients are compared with what is already in the fridge, freezer and cupboards. Only the missing items are added.
  2. It fills from the meal plan. Plan the week's dinners and all the gaps are collected in one list.
  3. It is shared with the household. Everyone sees the same list and can tick things off in the shop.
  4. It updates the inventory. Photograph the receipt after shopping and the items are registered back into your inventory.

You can also add things by hand, of course: washing-up liquid, coffee or the items that should always be in the house.

One list for the whole household

Cibello's household feature exists so that several people can work from the same food inventory, the same weekly meal plan and the same shopping list. Whoever is on the way home sees what is missing, and whoever did the planning does not have to send the list separately. During the free trial, and afterwards with a paid plan, the household shares one overview. The trial lasts 14 days and does not require a card.

Anyone in the household can add to the list, so the person who notices the coffee is running low is the one who writes it down, at the moment they notice. The list is the same on every phone, and an item ticked off in the shop disappears for everyone.

A list that cuts waste and cost

When the list only contains what is missing, most duplicate purchases disappear. When dishes that use up short-dated food are planned first, waste drops too. It is the same principle as in our guide to reducing food waste: overview before shopping. The saving is not dramatic on any single trip, but a jar of pesto you did not need to buy and a bag of spinach you actually ate add up over a year.

It also changes how you shop. A list you trust is one you follow, and that is where the impulse purchases and the third bag of salad tend to disappear. Shopping becomes a shorter, calmer errand rather than a walk round every aisle in case something is needed. The list also tells you when to stop, which no amount of good intentions in the bread aisle ever does.

After the shop: closing the loop

A shopping list is only half the system. The other half is what happens when the bags come home. In Cibello you can photograph the receipt or scan barcodes so the new items land in the inventory, ready to be matched against recipes for the rest of the week. You check the result before saving, because AI can misread a receipt line, and quantities and dates are yours to correct. From then on the pantry knows what arrived, and tonight's suggestions can use it.

A quiet week, in practice

Sunday: you review the suggested plan for the week and swap one dish. The list fills itself with what is missing. Monday: someone shops on the way home, ticking items off on their phone. The receipt is photographed at the kitchen table. Wednesday: the app notes that the chicken should be used soon and suggests it for Thursday. Friday: nothing is thrown away, and nobody has stood in the supermarket wondering whether there is still rice at home.

Frequently asked questions

Can the whole household see the same shopping list?

Yes. The household feature in Cibello shares the food inventory, meal plan and shopping list between members, during the trial and afterwards with a paid plan.

Does the shopping list fill itself automatically?

Yes. When you plan recipes or a weekly menu, the ingredients missing from your inventory are added to the list.

Can I add my own items to the list?

Yes, anything can be added by hand, including items that are not food.

Is the inventory updated after I shop?

You can photograph the receipt or scan barcodes so the items are added to your inventory. Check the result, since AI can misread.
